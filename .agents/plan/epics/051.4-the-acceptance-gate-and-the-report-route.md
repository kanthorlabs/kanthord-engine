# EPIC 051.4 — The ordered acceptance gate and the report route

Status: **draft**. It follows EPIC 051.3 by sequence order, and it runs before EPIC 052. It consumes EPIC 051.1's ingestion, discard and verdicts, EPIC 051.2's command gate, EPIC 051.3's land and checkpoint, and EPIC 050.2's authority prelude.

It carries a decimal number for the reason EPIC 051 states. It is the last epic of the family, and it is the one that makes the capability reachable over the wire.

## Goal

A `node.report` on an execution node is judged by an ordered gate, and the daemon lands or refuses:

- `acceptExecution` runs the steps of `worker.md` section 8 in order — reachability, repository cardinality, ancestry, declared paths, commands, land — and the first failure names itself;
- the six refusal codes are `candidate-unreachable`, `multi-repository-unsupported`, `ancestry-broken`, `path-undeclared`, `command-failed` and `contended`, and every one is reachable over the real route;
- the candidate ref is deleted on acceptance, on rejection and on contention;
- `reportOutcome` calls `acceptExecution` as a nested callable after the authority prelude, and the handler keeps its one injected command;
- the reported repository joins the wire as a required request field, which the `/v1` policy already permits.

## Non-goals

- **No new seam of the units it composes.** Every token of the composed paths was introduced by EPIC 051.1, EPIC 051.2 or EPIC 051.3. This epic decides the order.
- **No structural or review acceptance.** EPIC 052 and EPIC 053 own them.
- **No aggregation and no parent state.** An accepted checkpoint on a task gives its own terminal state, and on an atomic objective it gives `awaiting_approval` under the `objective-land-accepted` trigger EPIC 051.3 adds. It does **not** reach that state through `objectiveOutcome` at `src/domain/outcome.ts:17`: that function projects a `TerminalState` from a node's children, and an atomic objective has none. Gate row 10 states the same thing.
- **No attempt-workspace disposal.** This range creates no attempt workspace, and `src/services/` holds no service that could remove one. The epic that creates one owns its disposal.

## Decisions

- **Acceptance is an ordered gate, and the first failure names itself.** `acceptExecution` runs the steps of `worker.md` section 8 in order: reachability, repository cardinality, ancestry, declared paths, commands, land. An earlier failure short-circuits, because running a command against a commit that is not a descendant of the base proves nothing.

- **Every refusal that has an existing candidate ref draws `candidate.discard`, and the missing-ref path does not**, because the ref never existed. That is why `report-refusal-candidate-unreachable` holds one step and every later refusal holds a discard as its last.

- **Ancestry is verified against the recorded base, not against the branch head.** The base is what the worker started from. A head that moved is the contention case, detected at the swap. Verifying ancestry against a moved head would report `ancestry-broken` for work that is merely late.

- **The two git reads that feed the pure verdicts live in `acceptExecution`.** `ancestryVerdict` and `declaredPathVerdict` of EPIC 051.1 receive values and read no git, so the ancestry query (`git.isAncestor`) and the name-only diff (`git.changedPaths`) sit at the one call site that exists to hold them.

- **The accepted land owns the terminal writes, and `reportOutcome` keeps none of them.** EPIC 051.3's `land.settle:accepted` closes the attempt, stamps the run head, ends the run, writes the node transition and appends `outcome.reported` and `run.ended`, because those effects must sit in the transaction that writes the checkpoint and `AGENTS.md` gives the journaled write no third transaction for a caller to use. Story 8 therefore **removes** the accepted arm of `src/commands/outcome/report-outcome.ts` — its `closeAttempt`, `stampRunHead`, `endRun`, `setNodeState` and `events.append` — and leaves the rejected, failed, cancelled and attempt-limit arms untouched. Two writers of one effect is the defect this avoids.

- **`acceptExecution` opens exactly two transactions, and it may not open a third.** The two are `land.begin` and `land.settle`, and the compare and swap sits between them in neither. `AGENTS.md` counts per command: `reportOutcome` opens one for its authority prelude, `acceptExecution` opens two as the journaled write, and no other command opens two. Ending the accepted run after the land would be a third, which is why EPIC 051.3 puts the attempt close, the run end and the two events inside `land.settle:accepted`.

- **The pid file is removed by this epic, outside every transaction.** `land.settle` returns the journal row's `child_token`, per EPIC 051.3, and `acceptExecution` passes it to `git.removePidFile` after the settle commits and before it returns or refuses. Clearing the column and leaving the file is a leak, and the removal is git I/O so it cannot sit in the settle.

- **`acceptExecution` is a nested callable inside `reportOutcome`, and the handler keeps its one command.** `AGENTS.md` requires a handler to parse, call exactly one command, and format, and `src/http/server/node/report-node.ts:11-12` injects exactly one callable today. `assertRunAuthority` is a **pure** function and it already runs inside the command: EPIC 050.2 Story 6 (`06-the-report-prelude`) omits it from its `Seams:` line for that reason and draws every step as `Command` to a service, and EPIC 050.4 Story 6 (`06-the-report-drops-the-lease`) reproduces that prefix. `reportOutcome` therefore runs the authority prelude in its own transaction, and then calls `acceptExecution`, which opens its own journaled begin and settle. `reportObjective` and `closeObjective` at `src/commands/outcome/report-outcome.ts:59-66` are the shape precedent, with one difference: both take the caller's transaction, and `acceptExecution` cannot, because it writes git.

- **The reported repository is a required request field, and the `/v1` policy already permits one.** `multi-repository-unsupported` compares the repository the report names against the run's sole `run_base` row, so the value must be on the wire or the refusal is unreachable. `nodeReportRequest`'s `accepted` member at `src/http/contract/outcome.ts:24-28` already carries `objectId`, which is the reported head, so only `repositoryId` is new. `docs/proposal/api/README.md:98` — `required` lists adding a required request field among the changes the daemon may make inside `/v1`, and `:110` — `moved` records the human ruling of 2026-09-03 that put it there and the condition under which it returns. **The amendment is already in the tree, so this epic writes none of it**; it adds the field under a permission that exists.

- **The permission makes the escape unnecessary, so this epic retires no capability and bumps no version.** EPIC 050.2 Story 8 (`08-the-policy-amendment-and-the-capability-swap`) replaces the "the list is closed" sentence with "closed by default", and gives a change **outside** the list one legal route: a recorded human ruling plus a capability retirement plus a major version. This epic's change is **inside** the list, so none of that applies. There is no capability swap, no `KANTHORD_VERSION` move, no compatibility-record row and no groundwork story. EPIC 050.4 and EPIC 050.5 each took a major version because each removed a field, and a removal of any kind is still forbidden.

- **The one wire signal this epic sends is the refusal, and that is enough.** A client that omits `repositoryId` gets the shipped invalid-request refusal, naming the missing field. `docs/proposal/api/README.md:110` states the condition on the permission — it holds only while no client outside this repository calls `/v1` — so the day that changes, the epic admitting that client moves the item back and this epic's field is one it must account for. That is recorded here so the accounting is findable.

- **The six refusal codes join `src/http/contract/errors.ts` and `src/cli/exit-code.ts` together.** A code in one table and not the other is a code the CLI cannot report. Story 9 carries both, with the operation's `errors` record.

- **A ref the worker wrote outside `refs/kanthord/candidate/` changes no outcome, and the assertion belongs here.** EPIC 051.1 states the reasoning and this epic is where the whole route exists to prove it. The case writes `refs/heads/<objectiveId>` directly, then reports, and asserts the daemon still lands by compare and swap from the recorded base. The assertion is that the daemon ignores the ref, not that the worker cannot write one.

- **The stale-fence refusal precedes every git read.** The authority prelude runs first and refuses before `acceptExecution` is called, so a report with a stale fence reaches no `Git` method.

- **Refusal precedence is proven by a decision table, not by a diagram.** A refusal is decided by predicates the recorder cannot see. Every pair of conditions that can trigger at once is asserted to report the earlier one, in `src/commands/checkpoint/accept-execution.test.ts`. The table drives the real gate and not the pure verdicts: `candidate-unreachable` is decided inside `ingest.candidate`, `command-failed` inside `commands.run` and `contended` by the compare and swap, and `src/domain/execution-acceptance.ts` exports none of the three.

## Sequence

This epic draws no diagram. A path is drawn by the story that changes it, under `.agents/plan/authoring.md`. The seven `acceptExecution` paths are written from nothing, so each ship diagram stands alone with an empty prior set. The `node.report` path is drawn by EPIC 050.4, so Story 8 declares `Supersedes: EPIC 050.4 report-lease-free` and carries no `baseline-` diagram.

### Seam keys this epic introduces

- `acceptExecution` — the nested command `reportOutcome` calls after the authority prelude. It is one step of the `node.report` diagram, and it carries its own seven diagrams.
- `execution.runBases(transaction, runId): readonly RunBaseRow[]` — new method on `Execution`. It is synchronous, like every other method of `src/services/execution/index.ts:95` — `Execution`, and `reportOutcome` calls it inside its authority prelude: `acceptExecution` opens no transaction of its own, so the read cannot sit there.
- `git.isAncestor`, `git.changedPaths` — introduced by EPIC 051.1; this epic is their caller in `acceptExecution`.
- `ingest.candidate`, `candidate.discard`, `commands.run`, `land.begin`, `land.settle:accepted`, `land.settle:contended` — introduced by EPIC 051.1, EPIC 051.2 and EPIC 051.3; this epic composes them.
- `git.refUpdate` — present since EPIC 006. It projects its `ref`, so the compare and swap draws the objective-branch alias and **not** a `:land` label. `.agents/plan/authoring.md` requires a projection to name a value the call receives, and `RefUpdateInput` at `src/services/git/index.ts:47` carries `gitDir`, `ref`, `expectedOid`, `nextOid` and `pidFile` and no field naming the call site. EPIC 051.3 states the same correction and hands it here, because this epic holds the only call site.
- **`acceptExecution` is the journaled write of this family, and it holds the two transactions.** EPIC 051.3 declares `land.begin` and `land.settle` as nested unit functions injected into `AcceptExecutionDependencies`, and this epic decides the order in which they and the compare and swap run. There is no composed `land.execute` command: a command whose only work is to sequence three calls its one caller could sequence itself has no second reason to exist, and EPIC 051.3 draws the two units alone.

## Stories

Each entry is a name and the output it contributes. The story file holds the change, the tasks and the diagrams, and it declares its kind.

1. **The gate refuses an unreachable candidate.** Add `src/commands/checkpoint/accept-execution.ts` and draw `report-refusal-candidate-unreachable`: one `ingest.candidate` step and the refusal. Both inner paths of the nested unit produce the same outer token at the same position, so the outer diagram is one. `story-implement`.

2. **The gate refuses a foreign repository.** Draws `report-refusal-multi-repository`. Adds `execution.runBases` and calls `repositoryVerdict`. The fixture records exactly one base row, because no code path produces two. `story-implement`.

3. **The gate refuses broken ancestry.** Draws `report-refusal-ancestry-broken`. Adds the `git.isAncestor` call of the recorded base against the candidate head. `story-implement`.

4. **The gate refuses an undeclared path.** Draws `report-refusal-path-undeclared`. Adds the `git.changedPaths` name-only diff that feeds `declaredPathVerdict`. `story-implement`.

5. **The gate refuses a failed command.** Draws `report-refusal-command-failed`. Adds the `commands.run` step. `story-implement`.

6. **The gate refuses a contended land.** Draws `report-refusal-contended`. Adds the three land steps and the trailing discard. `story-implement`.

7. **The gate accepts and writes the checkpoint.** Draws `report-execution-checkpoint`, the success path. Its prior set is empty, so it declares `Seams:` with every token signed `+`; the composed-story exemption of `.agents/plan/authoring.md` covers a diagram whose every step is a nested command, and four of its steps are direct seam calls of `acceptExecution`. Its scenario drives `acceptExecution` directly, not the `node.report` route. `story-implement`.

8. **The report route enforces the gate.** Wire `acceptExecution` into `reportOutcome` as a nested callable running after the authority prelude, remove the accepted arm's five writes from `reportOutcome` because `land.settle:accepted` now owns them, and leave the handler with its one injected command. Draw the `node.report` path with `Supersedes: EPIC 050.4 report-lease-free`. Map each refusal to its contract error in `src/http/server/`. `story-implement`.

9. **The contract and the proposal.** Add `repositoryId` to `nodeReportRequest`'s `accepted` member, add the six refusal codes to `src/http/contract/errors.ts`, `src/cli/exit-code.ts` and the operation's `errors` record, and regenerate `src/http/contract/field-decisions.fixture.ts`. The `/v1` policy needs no edit: `docs/proposal/api/README.md:98` already permits the required field. Add `docs/proposal/phase-2/checkpoints.md` stating the candidate ref contract, the checkpoint schema, the ordered acceptance gate, the three compare-and-swap fields, the contention lifecycle, the per-command immutable checkout, the exact-path rule, the empty-`paths` rule, the workspace head advance, the journal recovery rule and the candidate sweep. `story-foundation`.

- **EPIC 053** — it gains a story that lifts EPIC 050.1's `review-head-unavailable` guard and pins the judged checkpoint at claim. This family drops the lift, and EPIC 053 has no story touching `claim-node.ts`, `ClaimRefusal` or the refusal ordering today, so without that story a review claim stays refused forever. EPIC 053 must also rule whether the checkpoint is pinned at claim or named at report: naming it at report lets a reviewer choose among the accepted checkpoints of its `depends_on` set, which is the retrospective selection EPIC 050.1 pinned at claim to prevent. **The default if no ruling arrives: review claims stay refused**, which costs the product no shipped behaviour, because none exists.
