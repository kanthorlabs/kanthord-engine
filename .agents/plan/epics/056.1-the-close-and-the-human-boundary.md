## Goal

A human closes an objective against the evidence it names, and only a human:

- the close is the routed `node.approve` operation, and the human close leaves `node.report` entirely;
- an atomic objective names the accepted checkpoint it approves, and a stale reference is refused;
- a parent objective names the plan revision it read, and a moved graph is refused;
- a close writes the objective's terminal state and rolls the initiative up in the same transaction;
- `node.claim` and `node.report` admit a harness actor and a grant caller, and `node.switch` and `node.approve` admit a human alone.

## Non-goals

- **No new operation.** `src/http/contract/integration.ts:14` — `node.approve` already declares the human-only `POST /node/{node}/approve`, with `status: "stubbed"`. The close routes that operation. A `node.close` name was drafted, and adding it would leave two human terminalization operations on one node.
- **No new verb.** `close` stays the domain action, the command name and the event reason. Only the transport is `node.approve`.
- **No new command file, and no wrapper.** `closeObjective` becomes the transaction owner in place, per the decision below. A second command that only opened the transaction would put two files under one verb.
- **No publish, and no integration.** A close moves the node state. Remote origin is EPIC 113, and the merge is EPIC 112.
- **No approval evidence bundle.** `node.approvalEvidence` stays `stubbed`. EPIC 112 owns the bundle a human reads; this epic owns the reference the close carries.
- **No provenance columns, and no migration.** `node.closed_at` and `node.closed_actor` are built by no epic. The precedence over aggregation comes from the state guard, and the event row carries the actor.
- **No new event type.** The shipped `closePayload` already records a human close, and this epic extends it and widens the payload union of one shipped type rather than registering a `node.closed` type.
- **No switch.** EPIC 056 owns `node.switch`, its contract row and its `allowedActors`.

## Decisions

- **The close is `node.approve` moving from `stubbed` to `routed`, and the `closed` report member is removed.** `src/http/contract/integration.ts:14` — `node.approve` declares `allowedActors: ["human"]`, `idempotency: "memory"` and `replayable: [200]`, and those become the close's semantics. `src/http/contract/outcome.ts:146` — `idempotency` already declares the same pair on `node.report`, so the repointing costs the CLI no idempotency change. The human close is the `closed` member of `src/http/contract/outcome.ts:50` — `closed` today, reached through `src/commands/outcome/report-outcome.ts:63` — `closeObjective`. That member is deleted and the callable leaves `ReportOutcomeDependencies`. One canonical human route remains, which is what lets the next decision narrow `node.report`.

- **`closeObjective` becomes a top-level command, and `storage.transact` becomes step 1 of every close path.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:55-59` takes `(dependencies, transaction, input)` and holds no `storage` key, because `src/commands/outcome/report-outcome.ts:107` — `transact` opens the transaction and passes it down. Deleting the `closed` member leaves `closeObjective` with exactly one caller, the new handler, and `AGENTS.md` admits no transaction in a handler. It therefore gains `storage: Storage` and opens its own transaction, in the idiom of `src/commands/node/unblock-node.ts:54` — `transact`, which is the other human-only top-level command. **`+storage.transact` is consequently a token of all eight live diagrams of this epic, of none of the four baselines it draws, and of the EPIC 055.1 diagram its success story supersedes**, and every story declares it. `+storage.transact` is the only moved token of `close-actor-forbidden`, `close-refusal-node` and `close-refusal-run`; `close-refusal-aggregation` also gains `plan.newestRevision`, because the refusal order puts `graph-moved` ahead of it. Nothing else moves: `authoring.md` rules that inserting one step renumbers later ordinals and moves nothing, so the renumbered tokens stay context.

- **The actor boundary is registry data, and every direction refuses with `actor-forbidden`.** `src/http/server/authorize.ts:11` — `allowedActors` already refuses an actor kind an operation does not admit. `node.claim` at `src/http/contract/execution.ts:310` — `allowedActors` and `node.report` at `src/http/contract/outcome.ts:145` — `allowedActors` both declare `["human", "harness"]` today, and each becomes `["harness", "grant"]`: the human is removed and the grant caller `.agents/plan/epics/055.1-the-grant-governs-the-run.md:53` — `allowedActors` adds is kept. `worker.md:494` states "A human never claims and never reports." `node.switch` and `node.approve` already declare `["human"]`. Bespoke `human-cannot-claim`, `human-cannot-report` and `human-only-operation` codes were drafted; they name no condition a client acts on differently, they would turn registry data into authorization logic with its own paths to prove, and `AGENTS.md` requires route lifecycle to be registry data rather than a branch in a handler.

- **The narrowing lands in this epic because `closed` is the human's only reachable member of `node.report`.** `worker.md:494` states "A human never claims and never reports", which is the authority for removing the human. The _timing_ comes from the schema: five of the six members of `src/http/contract/outcome.ts:23` — `nodeReportRequest` carry a `fence` — `:26`, `:31`, `:36`, `:41` and `:46` — and only `src/http/contract/outcome.ts:50` — `closed` does not. `.agents/plan/epics/050.1-the-claim.md:67` rules that a refusal never returns the fence and that the claim returns it to the claiming worker, so a fence is something only the run holder has. `closed` is therefore the only member a human can send, and narrowing `node.report` before deleting `closed` would leave EPIC 056 as a whole epic in which no human can terminalize an objective. **`node.claim` is not under that constraint and stays here anyway**, because `worker.md:494` states one rule over both verbs, this epic is where that rule is cited, and the CLI consequence of row 27 is one consequence of one change. Splitting the pair across two epics would leave neither epic's Decisions stating the whole rule.

- **The boundary is six values across four operations, and the grant caller supplies two of them.** `src/domain/actor.ts:6` — `registeredActorKinds` holds exactly `human` and `harness`, and `src/http/server/variables.ts:11` — `actor` types the middleware variable as an `ActorRow`. `.agents/plan/epics/055.1-the-grant-governs-the-run.md:53` — `allowedActors` adds `grant` as a third `allowedActors` value, on `node.claim`, `node.report` and `node.renew` and on nothing else, and `.agents/plan/epics/055.1-the-grant-governs-the-run.md:51` — `allowedActors` states that `src/http/server/authorize.ts:11` — `allowedActors` refuses a grant caller on every other operation. **This epic removes the human from two operations and keeps the grant caller on both.** A narrowing to `["harness"]` alone would refuse every external claim and report, which is the ask `.agents/plan/epics/055.1-the-grant-governs-the-run.md:53` — `allowedActors` made of this epic and which this epic takes. The six values are `node.claim` and `node.report` at `["harness", "grant"]`, and `node.switch` and `node.approve` at `["human"]`. **No case presents a `daemon` actor**: `src/services/storage/migration-0005-actor.ts:23` — `actor_kind` admits `daemon` on an event row, and `registeredActorKinds` admits no such actor.

- **The `actor-forbidden` guard inside `closeObjective` stays.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:62` — `harness` refuses a harness actor inside the command. The middleware now refuses the same caller earlier, so the guard is unreachable over the route and reachable from a direct call. It is defence at the command boundary, and `AGENTS.md` puts the domain rule in the command rather than in the transport.

- **A close names exactly one reference, and the request is a discriminated union over which.** `worker.md:550` states "The human closes the objective against the accepted checkpoint", and without a reference a close approves whatever landed after the human looked. The request is a `z.discriminatedUnion("reference", …)` in the idiom of `src/http/contract/outcome.ts:23` — `nodeReportRequest`:

  | member                    | fields                           | objective shape |
  | ------------------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------- |
  | `reference: "checkpoint"` | `checkpointId`, `outcome`        | `atomic`        |
  | `reference: "revision"`   | `revision`, `acknowledgePartial` | `parent`        |

  The union is what settles three things a pair of optional fields cannot. Exactly one reference always arrives, so no refusal is needed for a missing one. `outcome` appears only where it decides anything. And `acknowledgePartial` — required by `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:31` — `acknowledgePartial`, refused at `:117` and recorded in `closePayload` — stays in the request, where a flat `{ outcome, checkpointId?, revision? }` shape had dropped it while keeping the refusal that reads it.

- **The objective's shape decides which member is legal, and a mismatch refuses `reference-mismatch`.** `.agents/plan/epics/047-the-deliverable-and-the-node-pair.md:27` — `nodePairLegality` returns `shape: "parent" | "atomic"` from `(kind, deliverable)`, and `:33` gives the atomic-objective rows. The close reads `node.deliverable`, calls that pure function, and refuses `reference-mismatch` when the member does not match the shape. **The shape is never inferred from the request or from a child count**: inferring it from the request would let a human route a parent objective onto the atomic path by naming a checkpoint, and a child count is unavailable on the atomic path, which reaches `plan.readAllNodes` zero times.

- **A moved reference is refused, and each shape has its own read.** An atomic objective refuses `checkpoint-stale` when the named checkpoint is not the newest accepted one, read through `execution.newestAcceptedCheckpoint` of EPIC 053.1. A parent objective holds no attestation of its own, per `worker.md:371`, so it names the project's plan revision the human read, and the close refuses `graph-moved` when `plan.newestRevision` at `src/services/plan/index.ts:80` — `newestRevision` has moved on.

- **`outcome` is authoritative on the atomic path and absent from the parent request.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:115` — `aggregate` derives the parent value from the children, and every write, event type and returned field on that path uses the derived value. A parent `outcome` would be a second source for one written value, and `acknowledgePartial` already carries the only human decision that path admits. On the atomic path there are no children to aggregate, so the human states the value; it must be one of `src/domain/state.ts:20` — `terminalStates`, and a member carrying `awaiting_approval` refuses `outcome-not-terminal` after the node read.

- **The shipped close serves a parent objective and demands an atomic objective's attestation, and that contradiction is the repair this epic makes.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:108` — `readAllNodes` aggregates the objective's children and throws a bare `Error` at `:113` when there are none, so the command only ever served a parent objective. `:99` refuses `object-not-attested` when `run.headOid` is null, which is an attestation a parent objective never holds per `worker.md:371`. The parent path therefore drops the `headOid` demand and takes the `revision` reference, and the atomic path is written from nothing.

- **`closePayload.objectId` becomes nullable, and that is forced by dropping the `headOid` demand.** `src/http/contract/event-payload.ts:37` — `closePayload` declares `objectId` non-nullable, while `:56` — `headOid` shows `verdictPayload` spelling the nullable form. `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:151` writes `objectId: run.headOid` into the event, so a parent close whose run holds a null `headOid` — the very path this epic enables — would throw at the append. The field becomes nullable, it is null on the parent path, and the atomic path carries the `headOid` of the checkpoint it approved. The response needs no change: `src/domain/outcome-report.ts:91` — `objectId` already types it `string | null`.

- **`discarded` needs a third trigger and a widened payload union, and neither is a new event type.** `src/http/contract/event-payload.ts:124` — `node.discarded` maps to `rollUpPayload` alone, while `:125` and `:130` map `node.done` and `node.partial` to `z.union([closePayload, rollUpPayload])`; a human close of a `discarded` outcome would fail that strict schema. `src/domain/external-transition.ts:9` — `human-close` and `:10` — `human-close-partial` are the only two triggers, `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:135` maps every non-`done` result to `human-close-partial`, and `src/domain/external-transition.ts:225` — `human-close` registers `close-objective.ts` as their writer. So `node.discarded` gains `closePayload` in its union, a `human-close-discarded` trigger joins the tuple with its transition row and its writer registration, and the trigger is chosen from the written state rather than from a two-way branch.

- **An `outcome` of `discarded` is legal on the atomic path only.** The atomic member is the only one that carries `outcome`, and `.agents/plan/epics/053-node-state-ownership.md:44` rules that an all-`discarded` parent objective reaches `discarded` by aggregation with no human. A parent close therefore never writes `discarded` by request, and `projection-discarded` stays the refusal for a parent whose children project it.

- **`projection-discarded` and `acknowledgement-required` are reachable, and a post-approval child move is what reaches them.** `worker.md:371` states a parent objective reaches `awaiting_approval` only "when `aggregate` gives `done`", so at that moment neither refusal can hold. `.agents/plan/epics/053-node-state-ownership.md` rules that `aggregateObjective` writes only from `running`, so a later child discard cannot move an `awaiting_approval` parent. The parent therefore stays `awaiting_approval` while its projection becomes `partial` or `discarded`, and the human close is what discovers it. **The fixture is that sequence, never a manufactured row**: children terminal and `done`, the parent moved to `awaiting_approval`, then a `node.discard` of one child or of all of them.

- **The closed set is `done`, `partial` and `discarded`.** `src/domain/state.ts:20` — `terminalStates` already fixes the tuple.

- **A close beats an aggregation through the state guard, and `.agents/plan/epics/053-node-state-ownership.md:68` owns that ruling.** This epic adds no mechanism and restates no argument: the precedence is a property of the transition table plus `src/services/plan/index.ts:119` — `setNodeState` validating the declared `from`, and no function reads a provenance column.

- **The close calls the shipped roll-up unconditionally, and it changes no line of it.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:157` — `aggregateInitiative` already runs inside the close transaction, which is the resumption EPIC 053 states its aggregation stops for, and `:158` already passes `initiativeId: node.parentId`, which is nullable. Both paths therefore call it exactly once, including for an objective with no parent, where the null is the no-op. **A conditional call would give one story two seam traces**, because a zero-call case and a one-call case are two diagrams, and one implement story draws one path.

- **The close refusals and their order:** `actor-forbidden`, `invalid-request`, `node-not-found`, `not-an-objective`, `not-awaiting-approval`, `reference-mismatch`, `outcome-not-terminal`, `no-active-run`, `internal-driver`, `checkpoint-stale` or `graph-moved`, `acknowledgement-required`, `projection-discarded`. The first is decided by the middleware and the second by the request schema, both before the command runs; the next five stop after the node read, the next two after the run read, the reference refusals after their own read, and the last two after the child read.

- **Six refusals split out of the shipped `illegal-transition`, and that code survives on `node.report`.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:36` — `CloseObjectiveRefusal` holds three values today, and six of this epic's refusals are one coarse `illegal-transition` carrying a `details.guard` string: `no-active-run` at `:89`, `internal-driver`'s detail at `:96`, `object-not-attested` at `:103` and `projection-discarded` at `:127`, plus the absent node at `:72` and the wrong state at `:78`. Nothing reads a `details.guard` value programmatically — the three sites construct it and no consumer branches on it — so the details shape is deleted with the code that carried it.

- **The success path supersedes EPIC 055.1's diagram, and the four refusal paths draw baselines.** `.agents/plan/epics/055.1-the-grant-governs-the-run.md:71` — `close-objective-root-grants` is the first drawing of the close **success** path, so `authoring.md` forbids a second `baseline-` diagram for it: Story 8 declares `Supersedes:` naming that diagram, draws no baseline, and carries the grant-root seams as context. EPIC 055.1 drew no refusal path, so `baseline-close-actor-forbidden`, `baseline-close-node`, `baseline-close-run` and `baseline-close-aggregation` are first drawings of four paths nobody has drawn, and Stories 2, 3, 4 and 7 declare `Baselines:`. **EPIC 055.1 asked for the opposite and that ask is deleted, not disputed.** It asked Stories 4, 7 and 8 all to supersede `close-objective-root-grants`. - **The atomic path carries the grant-root effect, and no baseline measures the omission.** EPIC 055.1 Story 7 adds the effect to the one close path that existed then.
