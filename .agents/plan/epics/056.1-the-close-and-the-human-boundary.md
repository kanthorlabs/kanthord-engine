# EPIC 056.1 — The close and the human boundary

Status: **draft**. It follows EPIC 056 by sequence order, and it runs before EPIC 057. It consumes EPIC 056's `node.switch` operation, EPIC 053's parent-objective aggregation, EPIC 053.1's judged-checkpoint rule, and EPIC 055's caller kinds.

It carries a decimal number because EPIC 057 is already authored, and renumbering it would break every cross-reference to it.

**Dispatch prerequisite.** EPIC 056 ships before this one, because the actor narrowing needs `node.switch` to exist. Story 1 inserts `"056.1"` into `scripts/epic-sequence-range.ts:1` — `authoredEpics` after `"056"`, and Story 10 appends it to `shippedEpics`, which `test/sequence/conformance.test.ts:254` — `shippedEpics` requires to stay a prefix of `authoredEpics`. Story 10 is therefore last in dispatch order.

## Goal

A human closes an objective against the evidence it names, and only a human:

- the close is the routed `node.approve` operation, and the human close leaves `node.report` entirely;
- an atomic objective names the accepted checkpoint it approves, and a stale reference is refused;
- a parent objective names the plan revision it read, and a moved graph is refused;
- a close writes the objective's terminal state and rolls the initiative up in the same transaction;
- `node.claim` and `node.report` admit a harness actor alone, and `node.switch` and `node.approve` admit a human alone.

## Non-goals

- **No new operation.** `src/http/contract/integration.ts:14` — `node.approve` already declares the human-only `POST /node/{node}/approve`, with `status: "stubbed"`. The close routes that operation. A `node.close` name was drafted, and adding it would leave two human terminalization operations on one node.
- **No new verb.** `close` stays the domain action, the command name and the event reason. Only the transport is `node.approve`.
- **No publish, and no integration.** A close moves the node state. Remote origin is EPIC 113, and the merge is EPIC 112.
- **No approval evidence bundle.** `node.approvalEvidence` stays `stubbed`. EPIC 112 owns the bundle a human reads; this epic owns the reference the close carries.
- **No provenance columns, and no migration.** `node.closed_at` and `node.closed_actor` are built by no epic. The precedence over aggregation comes from the state guard, and the event row carries the actor.
- **No new event type.** The shipped `closePayload` already records a human close, and this epic extends it rather than registering a `node.closed` type.
- **No switch.** EPIC 056 owns `node.switch`, its contract row and its `allowedActors`.

## Decisions

- **The close is `node.approve` moving from `stubbed` to `routed`, and the `closed` report member is removed.** `src/http/contract/integration.ts:14` — `node.approve` declares `allowedActors: ["human"]`, `idempotency: "memory"` and `replayable: [200]`, and those become the close's semantics. The human close is the `closed` member of `nodeReportRequest` today, reached through `src/commands/outcome/report-outcome.ts:63` — `closeObjective`. That member is deleted, the callable leaves `ReportOutcomeDependencies`, and `node.approve` invokes `closeObjective` directly. One canonical human route remains, which is what lets the next decision narrow `node.report`.

- **The actor boundary is registry data, and every direction refuses with `actor-forbidden`.** `src/http/server/authorize.ts:11` — `allowedActors` already refuses an actor kind an operation does not admit. `node.claim` at `src/http/contract/execution.ts:310` — `allowedActors` and `node.report` at `src/http/contract/outcome.ts:145` — `allowedActors` both declare `["human", "harness"]` today, and each becomes `["harness"]`. `node.switch` and `node.approve` already declare `["human"]`. Bespoke `human-cannot-claim`, `human-cannot-report` and `human-only-operation` codes were drafted; they name no condition a client acts on differently, they would turn registry data into authorization logic with its own paths to prove, and `AGENTS.md` requires route lifecycle to be registry data rather than a branch in a handler. The four directions are therefore four values and one shipped refusal code.

- **The `actor-forbidden` guard inside `closeObjective` stays.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:62` — `harness` refuses a harness actor inside the command. The middleware now refuses the same caller earlier, so the guard is unreachable over the route and reachable from a direct call. It is defence at the command boundary, and `AGENTS.md` puts the domain rule in the command rather than in the transport.

- **A close names the evidence it approves, and a moved evidence is refused.** `worker.md` section 10 states the human closes the objective against the accepted checkpoint. The request carries `{ outcome, checkpointId?, revision? }`. An atomic objective supplies `checkpointId`, and the close refuses `checkpoint-stale` when that checkpoint is not the newest accepted one, read through `execution.newestAcceptedCheckpoint` of EPIC 053.1. A parent objective holds no attestation of its own, per `worker.md:371`, so it supplies `revision`, the project's plan revision id the human read, and the close refuses `graph-moved` when `plan.newestRevision` has moved on. Without a reference, a close approves whatever landed after the human looked.

- **`outcome` is a terminal state, and the closed set is `done`, `partial` and `discarded`.** `src/domain/state.ts:20` — `terminalStates` already fixes the tuple. A close carrying `awaiting_approval` refuses `outcome-not-terminal`, after the node read, because the refusal order places it there.

- **The shipped close serves a parent objective and demands an atomic objective's attestation, and that contradiction is the repair this epic makes.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:108` — `readAllNodes` aggregates the objective's children and throws when there are none, so the command only ever served a parent objective. `:99` refuses `object-not-attested` when `run.headOid` is null, which is an attestation a parent objective never holds per `worker.md:371`. The parent path therefore drops the `headOid` demand and takes the `revision` reference, and the atomic path is written from nothing.

- **A close beats an aggregation through the state guard.** `.agents/plan/epics/053-node-state-ownership.md` rules that `aggregateObjective` writes only from `running`, and `src/services/plan/index.ts:119` — `setNodeState` validates the declared `from` against the stored state. A closed objective is `done`, `partial` or `discarded`, so no later aggregation can reach it, and no read of a provenance column enforces the precedence.

- **The close calls the shipped roll-up, and it changes no line of it.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:157` — `aggregateInitiative` already runs inside the close transaction, which is the resumption EPIC 053 states its aggregation stops for. `worker.md:367` states human input overrides aggregation, and this is where the human input re-enters the walk.

- **The CLI keeps `node close`, and only the operation it calls changes.** `src/cli/inventory.ts:56` — `close` declares the command path `["node", "close"]` with `operationIds: ["node.report"]`, and `src/cli/node/close.ts` builds the request. Deleting the `closed` member makes that call unroutable, so the command names `node.approve` instead and gains the `--checkpoint` and `--revision` options the reference requires. `.agents/plan/epics/040-project-management-cli-ergonomics.md:49` states `node attest` and `node close` remain the two objective spellings, and this epic keeps both spellings.

- **The close refusals and their order:** `actor-forbidden`, `node-not-found`, `not-an-objective`, `not-awaiting-approval`, `outcome-not-terminal`, `no-active-run`, `internal-driver`, `checkpoint-stale` or `graph-moved`, `acknowledgement-required`, `projection-discarded`. The first is decided by the middleware, the next four stop after the node read, the next two after the run read, the reference refusals after their own read, and the last two after the child read.

- **The close event keeps its shipped type and gains the reference it approved.** `src/commands/outcome/close-objective.ts:141` — `events.append` writes `node.done` or `node.partial` with the `closePayload` of `src/http/contract/event-payload.ts:37` — `closePayload`, carrying `reason: "human-close"`. The payload gains `checkpointId` and `revision`, both nullable, so the event records which reference the human approved. `discarded` joins the type set as `node.discarded`, because `outcome` admits it and `src/domain/event-type.ts:10` — `node.discarded` already exists. The actor is on the event row, so no column records who closed it.

## Stories

Each entry is a name and the output it contributes. The story file holds the change, the tasks and the diagrams, and it declares its kind. `.agents/plan/authoring.md` is the standard.

1. **The close contract and the actor boundary.** Move `node.approve` to `status: "routed"` with its request and response schemas and its `errors` record, delete the `closed` member from `nodeReportRequest`, set `allowedActors` to `["harness"]` on `node.claim` and `node.report`, add the refusal codes to `src/http/contract/errors.ts` and `src/cli/exit-code.ts`, extend `closePayload` with the two nullable references, repoint `src/cli/inventory.ts:57` — `operationIds` and `src/cli/node/close.ts` at `node.approve` with its two new options, and regenerate `src/http/contract/field-decisions.fixture.ts`. Insert `"056.1"` into `authoredEpics`. `story-foundation`.

2. **A harness actor refuses before any read.** Draws `close-actor-forbidden`, superseding `baseline-close-actor-forbidden`: `clock.now` and the refusal, and nothing else. `story-implement`.

3. **A wrong node or a wrong outcome refuses after the node read.** Draws `close-refusal-node`, superseding `baseline-close-node`, adding `plan.readNode`. The four refusals `node-not-found`, `not-an-objective`, `not-awaiting-approval` and `outcome-not-terminal` stop at the same step, so they are one diagram. `story-implement`.

4. **A missing or internal run refuses after the run read.** Draws `close-refusal-run`, superseding `baseline-close-run`, adding `execution.activeRunOfNode`. The `object-not-attested` refusal of the baseline is removed, and the story cites its replacement. `story-implement`.

5. **A stale checkpoint refuses an atomic close.** Draws `close-refusal-checkpoint-stale`, adding `execution.newestAcceptedCheckpoint`. Its prior set is empty, because no shipped path reads a checkpoint. `story-implement`.

6. **A moved graph refuses a parent close.** Draws `close-refusal-graph-moved`, adding `plan.newestRevision`. Its prior set is empty. `story-implement`.

7. **An unacknowledged or all-discarded projection refuses after the child read.** Draws `close-refusal-aggregation`, superseding `baseline-close-aggregation`, adding `plan.readAllNodes`. Both refusals stop at the same step. `story-implement`.

8. **The close of a parent objective.** Draws `close-success-parent`, superseding `baseline-close-success`: the reads, `plan.setNodeState`, `events.append`, `aggregateInitiative` and `execution.endRun`. It drops the baseline's `object-not-attested` demand. Adds the handler at `src/http/server/node/approve-node.ts` and the `src/main.ts` binding, and removes the `closeObjective` callable from `ReportOutcomeDependencies`. `story-implement`.

9. **The close of an atomic objective.** Draws `close-success-atomic`, which reads the checkpoint instead of the children and reaches no `aggregateInitiative` when the parent is absent. Its prior set is empty. `story-implement`.

10. **The proposal records the close and the boundary.** Extend `docs/proposal/phase-2/human-controls.md` with the close reference contract and its two staleness refusals, the full refusal order, the precedence over aggregation and the state guard that carries it, the roll-up inside the close transaction, the removal of the `closed` report member, and the four actor directions as registry data. Append `"056.1"` to `shippedEpics`. It is last in dispatch order. `story-foundation`.

## Amendments this epic asks of other epics

None is applied here, and a human applies each before dispatch.

- **EPIC 053.1 Story 10 (`10-the-contract-the-cli-and-the-proposal`)** — it states the `review` member is the eighth of `nodeReportRequest`. This epic deletes the `closed` member, so the union holds seven after both land. That epic's count is correct when it ships and stale afterwards. A human either narrows the sentence to name the member set at its own time, or restates the count here. **The default if no ruling arrives: one epic states a member count the other contradicts**, and a reader cannot tell which is current.

- **EPIC 112** — `node.approvalEvidence` stays `stubbed`, and this epic routes `node.approve` beside it. That epic's bundle is the evidence a human reads before the close this epic implements, so it must state which reference the bundle returns for an atomic objective and which for a parent. **The default if no ruling arrives: the bundle names no reference**, and a human cannot supply the `checkpointId` or the `revision` the close requires.
