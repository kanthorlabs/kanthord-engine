# EPIC 054.3 — The report members pay their attempt

Status: **draft**. It follows EPIC 054.2 by sequence order, and it runs before EPIC 054.4. It consumes EPIC 054.1's `endAttempt`, EPIC 052.2's structural report member and EPIC 053.1's review report member.

It carries a decimal number because EPIC 055, EPIC 056 and EPIC 057 are already numbered, and renumbering them would move every cross-reference.

**Dispatch prerequisite.** EPIC 054.2 ships before this one. Story 2 inserts `"054.3"` into `scripts/epic-sequence-range.ts:1` — `authoredEpics` after `"054.2"`, and the proposal story appends it to `shippedEpics`, which `test/sequence/conformance.test.ts:254` — `shippedEpics` requires to stay a prefix of `authoredEpics`. The proposal story is therefore last in dispatch order.

**It holds ten stories: eight changed paths, the checkpoint derivation and the proposal.** Four of the eight are drawn here for the first time, because every diagram of `node.report` drawn so far covers an accepted arm; three are accepted arms, which close their attempt through `end-attempt` from this epic on; and the eighth is the run-scoped candidate discard, a nested command this epic creates and therefore draws. Ten is the cap of `.agents/plan/authoring.md:60` — `no more than ten stories`, and the discard story takes the tenth slot deliberately.

## Goal

A report that is not accepted charges the class the daemon observed:

- a worker's own failure report charges `worker-reported-failure`, which is semantic;
- a structural rejection and a review rejection each charge `daemon-rejected`, which is semantic;
- all three settle inside the prelude transaction `reportOutcome` already owns, and none opens a second;
- both failure reports discard their candidate ref after that transaction commits and before the reap, and the two rejections have none to discard;
- the accepted structural patch and both accepted review attestations close through the same command, so every close appends one event;
- every checkpoint writer records `caller` and `subject` from the same two sources as the attempt.

## Non-goals

- **No change to the report route's execution arm.** `report-lease-free` of EPIC 050.4 Story 6 (`06-the-report-drops-the-lease`) and `report-checkpoint-reap` of EPIC 051.6 Story 3 (`03-the-report-reaps-on-every-settled-terminal`) stay as drawn: `report-checkpoint-gate` already moved the execution close into the nested accept, and EPIC 054.2 owns that close. **The structural and the review accepted arms do change**, because each still closes its own attempt.
- **No termination on an accepted arm.** The three accepted stories change the caller of the close and nothing about the class: the row keeps a null `termination`, and the event records the acceptance with its `headOid`.
- **No refusal diagram of the two nested accepts moves.** `acceptStructural` and `acceptReview` take the caller's transaction and open none — `.agents/plan/epics/052.1-the-structural-acceptance.md:35` — `takes the caller's transaction` — so the termination write is one statement after the accept returns, and the eleven refusal diagrams of EPIC 052.1 and EPIC 053.1 keep their traces. **Eleven, not nine**: EPIC 052.1 draws seven and EPIC 053.1 draws four. Nine is EPIC 052.1's count of refusal _groups_ at `.agents/plan/epics/052.1-the-structural-acceptance.md:74` — `conditionality`, and a group is not a diagram. **Their scenario files do change**, because each catches the thrown error and returns it as `result`; a returned disposition is passed straight through, and `test/helpers/sequence-conformance.ts:303` — `resultTerminal` derives `refuse:<code>` from either shape, which is why the diagrams themselves hold.
- **No execution gate arm.** EPIC 054.2 owns the five gate refusals and the contended land.
- **No backfill.** A checkpoint written before this epic keeps a null caller and a null subject, for the reason the attempt row keeps them.
- **No new transaction on a charged report path.** The prelude transaction is open on all four charged paths, so this epic adds no `storage.transact` step to any of them. The run-scoped discard of Story 1 (`01-the-run-scoped-candidate-discard`) opens one of its own, outside every report transaction, because it reads the run's base homes and a git deletion may not sit inside a storage transaction.
- **No node transition and no run ending on a rejection arm.** `.agents/plan/epics/054.2-the-execution-accept-pays-its-attempt.md:50` — `A gate refusal writes no node state` already ruled it for this family: `src/commands/outcome/report-outcome.ts:247` — `taskReportEffect` is the only chooser of `ready` or `blocked` and it runs on a worker-report body alone, so a refused structural or review report leaves the run `active`, the node `running` and no open attempt, and `src/commands/run/expire-runs.ts:30` — `expireDueRuns` recovers the node at the run's deadline because it reads no attempt row. Adding a transition would put `plan.setNodeState` and `execution.endRun` on both rejection diagrams and give the daemon a second writer of a terminal the report path does not own. **Whether a worker may hand back a run it can no longer report on is the one open product question, and EPIC 054.2's story tree is where it is recorded** — not here, and not three times.

## Decisions

- **The four charged paths settle in `reportOutcome`'s prelude transaction.** `.agents/plan/epics/051.4-the-acceptance-gate-and-the-report-route.md:40` — `reportOutcome` states the prelude transaction commits before the git-bearing accept runs, and the failure member, the structural member and the review member all reach their verdict inside it. A second transaction is refused, because `AGENTS.md` names the two-unjournaled-transactions shape a defect it intends to repair.

- **A rejection is a returned disposition here too, and for the same reason as EPIC 054.2.** A throw inside `storage.transact` rolls back the settlement. `acceptStructural` and `acceptReview` return the rejection, `reportOutcome` writes the termination, commits, and then raises the refusal. `.agents/plan/epics/051.6-the-post-expiry-reap-on-the-run-operations.md:64` — `disposition` records that both shapes are legal.

- **These four paths are drawn for the first time, so each declares `Baselines:`.** `report-lease-free` draws the accepted arm — its `Seams:` line at `.agents/plan/stories/050.4-the-node-lease-removal/06-the-report-drops-the-lease.md:11` — `plan.setNodeState:T:outcome-accepted` holds the accepted node write — and every later report diagram supersedes an accepted arm. No epic has drawn a failure arm, so a `baseline-` diagram of shipped code is the prior set, and `.agents/plan/authoring.md:252` — `shipped code is drawn once` admits it, because no earlier epic drew that path.

- **A worker's own failure report is `worker-reported-failure`, and it is semantic.** The three worker-driven members `rejected`, `failed` and `cancelled` reach one arm at `src/commands/outcome/report-outcome.ts:234` — `closeAttempt`, and one diagram covers all three, because their **call set** and order are identical and only a label value differs. Their seam sets are **not** identical: `test/helpers/sequence-conformance.ts:51` — `plan.setNodeState` projects `[id, trigger]`, and `src/domain/outcome-report.ts:75` — `attempt-rejected` gives each member its own trigger, so the drawn token is one member's. The diagram is therefore drawn against a `failed` fixture, and the story states in prose that `rejected` and `cancelled` share the call set and are asserted by value in the command's own test. `.agents/plan/authoring.md:166` — `A branch that changes a value and not the call` is the rule, and `.agents/plan/stories/050.2-the-run-renew-release-and-report/05-the-release.md:16` — `projected.exhausted` is the precedent.

- **The worker arm is two drawn paths, and the attempt limit is what separates them.** `src/commands/outcome/report-outcome.ts:268` — `effect.runEnd` guards `execution.endRun`, and `src/domain/outcome-report.ts:71` — `runEnd: null` leaves it unreached below the limit. So the exhausted branch calls one seam the other does not, which is a **call-set** difference and not a value, and the rule above therefore makes it a second diagram. This is where the release precedent does not carry: `src/commands/node/release-node.ts:123` — `projected.exhausted` already called `endRun` on both branches, so its token sets were equal; here they are not.

- **A worker's claim of quota exhaustion is not `provider-quota`.** Only a response the daemon's own transport received carries a `responseHash`, so a worker that reports "out of quota" still pays a semantic attempt. This is the rule EPIC 054 settled, and this epic is where a caller could have broken it.

- **The failure report discards its candidate ref, and the structural and review rejections have none.** An execution run may hold `refs/kanthord/candidate/<runId>/<attemptNo>` when the worker reports a failure, and the discard is this epic's, after the report transaction commits. A **structural** report carries a patch and no object id, and a **review** claim writes no candidate ref at all — `.agents/plan/epics/053.1-the-review-checkpoint.md:72` — `claim-success-review` states it — so those two arms call `candidate.discardRun` zero times, and the case asserts the count rather than assuming it.

- **The six conditions hold for the one discard only once the sweep is attempt-aware.** The settlement commits before the deletion, so nothing rides with it; a failure leaves an inert ref. **Conditions 3 and 5 need a repair this epic asks for**: a below-limit worker failure closes attempt `n` and leaves the run `active`, and `.agents/plan/stories/051.1-the-candidate-and-the-git-primitives/08-the-candidate-namespace-is-enumerated.md:141` — `active.has(runId)` makes the startup sweep skip every ref of an active run, so nothing would retry a failed deletion while that run kept claiming attempts. The predicate that repairs both deletes a ref whose attempt number is **below** the run's open attempt, whatever the run state: an attempt below the open one is committed closed, which is the monotonic eligibility condition 3 requires, and the sweep is then the guaranteed retry condition 5 requires. The `## Amendments` section carries the ask, and Story 1 (`01-the-run-scoped-candidate-discard`) names it as a dispatch prerequisite. **The exhausted arm needs none of this**: an exhausted report ends the run, so the shipped predicate already covers it.

- **The reported run's own ref is a discard and never `candidate.reap`.** `.agents/plan/epics/051.6-the-post-expiry-reap-on-the-run-operations.md:29` — `No reap inside` states both, and a reviewer who reads the reap as having moved the discard is reading two calls as one.

- **The caller reaches the discard through a run-scoped nested unit, and `reportOutcome` gains no read.** `candidate.discard` takes `{ gitDir, ref }` — `.agents/plan/stories/051.1-the-candidate-and-the-git-primitives/04-the-candidate-ref-is-deleted.md:51` — `DiscardCandidateInput` — and `reportOutcome` holds no git seam and reads no repository home, so it cannot supply a `gitDir`. The `candidate` key therefore gains a second method beside `reap`:

  ```ts
  candidate: Readonly<{
    reap(runs: readonly ExpiredRun[]): Promise<unknown>;
    discardRun(
      input: Readonly<{ runId: string; attemptNo: number }>,
    ): Promise<unknown>;
  }>;
  ```

  `discardRun` resolves the run's base repository homes in its own transaction and calls `discardCandidate` per home over `candidateRef({ runId, attemptNo })`, exactly as `.agents/plan/stories/051.5-the-targeted-post-expiry-candidate-cleanup/05-an-ended-runs-candidate-ref-is-deleted.md:101` — `homes` already does for the reap. **The alternative was refused**: adding `execution.runBases:R` to the worker arm would put a seam call on the drawn path that `report-lease-free` never made, so the baseline would have to draw a step the shipped code does not take. The drawn token is the bare `candidate.discardRun`, because `test/helpers/sequence-conformance.ts:50` — `projections` declares no entry for it, and a bare token admits one call per diagram, which is all either worker diagram makes. **A multi-base run is handled by construction**, because the unit walks the home list rather than taking one `gitDir`.

- **The order after the commit is discard, then reap.** The operation's own ref goes first and the opportunistic sweep of other runs follows, which is the settle-then-discard order EPIC 054.2 states at `.agents/plan/stories/054.2-the-execution-accept-pays-its-attempt/08-the-ceiling-the-order-and-the-proposal.md:40` — `The discard order`. Both calls sit outside the transaction, so neither rides with the settlement, and `candidate.sweep` retries either. **The span count is unaffected**, because the case that counts transaction spans binds `candidate` to a double, as `.agents/plan/stories/054.2-the-execution-accept-pays-its-attempt/08-the-ceiling-the-order-and-the-proposal.md:118` — `Candidate` does; binding the real `discardRun` and the real `reap` would add two spans of their own and prove nothing about `reportOutcome`.

- **The two rejection arms reach `candidate.reap`, and the returned disposition is what makes that both possible and correct.** With the throw, an `AcceptStructuralError` or an `AcceptReviewError` raised inside the prelude rolled the transaction back and escaped the capture block that admits only `AcceptExecutionError`, so the reap never ran — `.agents/plan/stories/053.1-the-review-checkpoint/10-the-report-route-carries-a-verdict.md:69` — `AcceptReviewError` states it for the review arm and calls it the reason "every refusal diagram of Stories 3 to 6 ends before it". With the disposition the prelude commits, so the one reap statement runs before the raise. **It is also the first time the reap on those arms is correct**: the same story at `:229` — `candidate.reap` runs outside states that a reap over a rolled-back expiry pass would delete refs of runs still active. `+candidate.reap` is therefore a signed token on both rejection diagrams and not scope creep. **Suppressing it was refused**: a guard on the disposition would leave two arms of one command reaping and two not, for no product reason.

- **The run-scoped discard is a nested command, and it is drawn.** `candidate.discardRun` resolves the run's base homes in its own transaction and calls `discardCandidate` per home, so it is a path and not a helper: `.agents/plan/authoring.md:208` — `A nested command is one step` gives a nested command its own diagram and its own scenario, exactly as `reapRunCandidates` has EPIC 051.5's and `discardCandidate` has EPIC 051.1's. Story 1 (`01-the-run-scoped-candidate-discard`) owns `discard-run-candidate`, whose prior set is empty. **Folding it into the caller's story was refused**: that story would then change two paths, which `.agents/plan/authoring.md:147` — `More than one pair is too big` refuses, and the one unit whose failure leaks a git ref would be the one nothing replays.

- **`daemon-rejected` carries its refusal code.** `.agents/plan/epics/054-attempt-classification-and-the-supervisor.md:65` — `daemon-rejected` carries widens the member to `{ kind: "daemon-rejected"; refusal: string }`, because without it the ten structural codes, the five review codes and EPIC 054.2's five gate codes write one indistinguishable audit value and the code reaches the client and never the row. It is `string` and not a union of the three refusal types: `src/domain/termination.ts` is domain and may not import a command. **EPIC 054.2 must pass it too**, and the `## Amendments` section carries that ask.

- **The three accepted arms close through `end-attempt`, and their diagrams move by one token.** `.agents/plan/stories/052.1-the-structural-acceptance/09-the-accepted-patch.md:14` — `accept-structural-success`, `.agents/plan/stories/053.1-the-review-checkpoint/07-the-attestation-with-a-reason.md:13` — `accept-review-success-with-reason` and `.agents/plan/stories/053.1-the-review-checkpoint/08-the-attestation-with-no-reason.md:11` — `accept-review-success-no-reason` each hold `execution.closeAttempt:A`, and each becomes `attempt.end:A`. So the sign on each is `+attempt.end:A, -execution.closeAttempt:A`, exactly as EPIC 054.2 declares at `.agents/plan/stories/054.2-the-execution-accept-pays-its-attempt/07-the-accepted-land-closes-through-the-command.md:15` — `Seams`. No other step of any of the three moves, and no arm gains a transaction: `endAttempt` takes the one its caller already holds. **The token is `attempt.end`, and EPIC 054.2 owns its projection** at `.agents/plan/stories/054.2-the-execution-accept-pays-its-attempt/01-the-unreachable-candidate-pays-its-attempt.md:221` — `attempt.end`, so this epic adds no projection entry. EPIC 054 rules why an acceptance appends the event, and EPIC 054.1 rules that an accepted ending shares the seam set of a semantic one.

- **Every checkpoint writer records `caller` and `subject`, and this epic is their first filler.** `.agents/plan/epics/051.3-the-checkpoint-and-the-land.md:29` — `caller` created both columns nullable and named EPIC 054's family as their filler. `WriteCheckpointInput` gains both, and each of the three writers — the accepted land, the structural acceptance and the review attestation — passes `subject` from `run.worker` and `caller` from the authenticated principal. **This changes no drawn path**: `execution.writeCheckpoint` keeps its token and its projection, so no diagram of EPIC 051.3, EPIC 052.1 or EPIC 053.1 moves.
